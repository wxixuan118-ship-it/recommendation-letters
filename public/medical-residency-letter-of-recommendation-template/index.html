Writing guide

How to use a medical residency letter of recommendation template

A medical residency letter of recommendation template is an invaluable resource for attending physicians, clinical faculty, and medical students navigating the residency application process through the Electronic Residency Application Service (ERAS). Residency programs receive thousands of applications, and strong letters of recommendation are among the most important factors program directors consider when deciding who to invite for interviews. A well-structured template helps ensure that all the critical information programs look for is included.

Medical residency recommendation letters differ from standard academic or employment references in several important ways. They must address specific clinical competencies as defined by the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME). These include patient care, medical knowledge, practice-based learning and improvement, interpersonal and communication skills, professionalism, and systems-based practice. The strongest letters provide concrete clinical examples that demonstrate these competencies rather than simply listing them.

The writer of a medical residency recommendation letter should be someone who has directly supervised the applicant’s clinical work. Typically, this means an attending physician under whom the student completed a core clerkship or elective rotation. The letter should specify the rotation setting, duration, and the student’s role and responsibilities. According to the Association of American Medical Colleges, letters from clinical attendings who know the student well carry significantly more weight than those from research mentors or community physicians with limited clinical contact.

A strong medical residency letter of recommendation should include a comparative statement that places the applicant in context with their peers. For example, the writer might state that the applicant is in the top 5 percent, top 10 percent, or top quartile of medical students they have supervised over a given period. This ranking helps program directors understand how exceptional the applicant is relative to other candidates. However, the ranking must be honest and backed up with specific examples, as program directors can easily spot inflated praise.

Specific clinical anecdotes are the heart of a strong residency recommendation letter. Describe a particular patient case, clinical scenario, or team situation where the student demonstrated exceptional clinical reasoning, diagnostic skills, patient communication, or professionalism. The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) works well for structuring these examples. Be specific about what the student did, why it was notable, and what the outcome was. Avoid vague praise like "hard worker" or "team player" without supporting evidence.

The letter should also address any areas for growth or improvement, as long as they are framed constructively. Residency program directors appreciate honest assessments, and mentioning that a student has room to grow in a particular area actually makes the praise of their strengths more credible. However, if there are significant red flags that would make the student a poor fit for residency, the writer should consider whether they can honestly write a supportive letter at all.

Medical residency letters of recommendation are typically one to two pages in length and should follow a professional format. They should be on institutional letterhead, signed by the writer, and submitted through ERAS or the program’s preferred application system. Students should provide their letter writers with a copy of their CV, personal statement, and a summary of their relevant clinical experiences to help the writer craft a detailed, accurate letter.

When using a medical residency letter of recommendation template, it is important to remember that the letter represents you as a medical professional. Make sure the letter is professional, well-written, and free of errors. Ask a trusted mentor or colleague to review it before you submit it, if possible. The quality of your recommendation letters reflects on you as an applicant and on the physicians who wrote them, so taking the time to get them right is worth the effort.

Medical students should also think strategically about who they ask for recommendation letters. It is better to have a slightly less famous attending who knows you well and can write a detailed, enthusiastic letter, than a big name who can only write a generic, lukewarm one. Program directors can tell the difference, and a detailed letter from someone who worked closely with you will always be more impressive than a short form letter from a department chair you barely know.

Final review checklist

  • Writer’s clinical relationship to the applicant is clearly stated.
  • Rotation setting, duration, and responsibilities are specified.
  • Specific clinical examples demonstrate ACGME competencies.
  • A comparative ranking places the applicant among peers.
  • The letter is on letterhead, signed, and submitted through ERAS.

Frequently asked questions

How many letters of recommendation do I need for residency?

Most residency programs require three to four letters of recommendation. Typically, these should come from clinical attendings under whom you completed core clerkships or electives, plus potentially a research mentor if research is a significant part of your application.

What should a medical residency letter of recommendation include?

It should include the writer’s relationship to the applicant, the duration and setting of their supervision, specific clinical examples demonstrating key competencies, a comparative ranking relative to peers, and a clear statement of support for the candidate.

Who should write my medical residency letters of recommendation?

Choose attendings who know you well from clinical rotations, especially in specialties related to the program you are applying to. Research mentors can be good additional letter writers, but clinical attendings generally carry more weight for residency applications.
