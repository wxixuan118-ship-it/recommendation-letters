Based on my experience, I strongly recommend [Candidate Name] for [opportunity]. I am confident [he/she/they] will [expected outcome or contribution]. Please feel free to contact me at [email/phone] if you need additional information.

Writing guide

How to use a ask for a letter of recommendation template

An ask for a letter of recommendation template helps students, employees, and applicants write professional, effective requests for recommendation letters. Many people find it awkward or intimidating to ask someone to write them a recommendation, and having a well-crafted template makes the process easier and increases the likelihood of getting a strong, positive letter. The best requests are polite, clear, and provide everything the recommender needs to write a good letter.

The first rule of asking for a recommendation letter is to choose the right person. The best recommenders are people who know you well, who have worked with you closely, and who can speak to your specific strengths with concrete examples. A supervisor from a job where you excelled, a professor whose class you aced, or a mentor who has guided your development are all good choices. According to the College Board, the quality of your recommendation letters can significantly impact your college or graduate school application.

When asking for a letter of recommendation, timing is critical. You should ask at least four to six weeks before the deadline, and ideally even earlier. Busy professors, managers, and mentors receive many requests, and giving them plenty of time ensures they can write a thoughtful, detailed letter rather than a rushed one. If you need the letter by a specific date, state that clearly. It is also a good idea to send a gentle reminder a week or so before the deadline.

Your request should include several key pieces of information. First, state clearly what you are applying for and why you are asking this particular person. Second, provide a brief summary of your relevant achievements, experiences, or coursework with them, to jog their memory and give them material to work with. Third, attach or link to your resume or CV, your personal statement or application essay draft if you have one, and any other relevant materials. Fourth, explain how the letter should be submitted and by what deadline. Finally, offer to meet or provide more information if needed.

The tone of your request should be polite, professional, and appreciative. Acknowledge that the person is busy and that you appreciate their time and consideration. Do not assume they will say yes, and give them an easy way to decline gracefully. For example, you might say, "I completely understand if you are too busy to write a strong letter at this time." If someone says no, thank them anyway and ask someone else. A lukewarm letter from someone who does not really want to write it is worse than a strong letter from someone else.

There are several ways to ask for a recommendation letter. In person is often best for people you see regularly, as it allows for a conversation and shows respect. Email is also perfectly acceptable and gives the person time to consider and respond. For very formal situations or people you do not know well, a formal letter might be appropriate. Regardless of the method, follow up with a written request that includes all the details they will need, so they have a record to refer back to.

After someone agrees to write you a recommendation letter, make the process as easy as possible for them. Provide all requested materials promptly. Answer any questions they have. Send a reminder as the deadline approaches. Most importantly, send a thank-you note afterward, whether or not you get accepted to the program or get the job. Recommenders invest time and effort into writing letters, and acknowledging that effort is both polite and important for maintaining the relationship.

One common mistake people make when using a ask for a letter of recommendation template is sending the same generic request to everyone they ask. While a template can be a good starting point, you should always personalize each request to the specific person you are asking. Mention a specific shared experience, project, or class to jog their memory and show that you value their particular perspective. This personal touch makes it much more likely they will agree to write a strong, detailed letter.

If someone declines to write you a recommendation letter, thank them anyway and do not press them further. They may be too busy, or they may not feel they know you well enough to write a strong letter. Either way, pushing for a letter would only result in a weaker recommendation that does not help your application. Instead, focus on finding someone else who knows you well and can write you a genuinely enthusiastic letter.

Final review checklist

  • You have asked the right person who knows you well.
  • The request is made at least four to six weeks before the deadline.
  • All necessary materials (resume, personal statement, etc.) are provided.
  • Submission instructions and deadline are clearly stated.
  • A thank-you note is sent after the letter is submitted.

Frequently asked questions

How do I politely ask for a letter of recommendation?

Ask in person or by email, explain what you are applying for and why you are asking them specifically, provide all necessary materials, give plenty of notice, and express appreciation for their time and consideration.

How far in advance should I ask for a recommendation letter?

Ask at least four to six weeks before the deadline. For graduate school applications or busy periods like the end of a semester, ask even earlier, ideally two to three months in advance.

What should I provide with my request?

Provide your resume or CV, a draft of your personal statement or application essay if available, a brief summary of your relevant experiences with the recommender, the deadline, and submission instructions. Also mention specific points you hope they will address.
