Letter Of Recommendation Residency Template

Dear Program Director: It is with great enthusiasm that I recommend [Student Name] for your residency program.

I have known Letter for [number of years/months] as [relationship context]. During this time, I have consistently observed [key quality 1], [key quality 2], and a strong commitment to [relevant area].

One example that stands out is when [specific situation]. In this instance, [he/she/they] demonstrated [specific skill or quality] by [action taken]. The result was [measurable outcome or impact], which [broader significance].

Beyond [specific area], Letter consistently shows [personal quality 1] and [personal quality 2]. [Additional supporting detail or example]. These qualities make [him/her/them] an exceptional [candidate/student/employee/tenant].

Based on my experience, I strongly recommend [Candidate Name] for [opportunity]. I am confident [he/she/they] will [expected outcome or contribution]. Please feel free to contact me at [email/phone] if you need additional information.

Writing guide

How to use a letter of recommendation residency template

A letter of recommendation residency template provides a reliable structure for clinical faculty members who need to write strong, effective reference letters for medical students applying to residency programs. With the National Resident Matching Program (NRMP) processing tens of thousands of applications each year, program directors rely heavily on recommendation letters to identify candidates who will thrive in their programs. A well-structured template helps busy clinicians write detailed, substantive letters efficiently while ensuring all critical information is included.

The opening paragraph of a residency recommendation letter should establish the writer’s credentials, their relationship to the student, the clinical setting and duration of their interaction, and a clear statement of the strength of their recommendation. Program directors read many letters quickly, so a strong opening that conveys genuine enthusiasm and specific context grabs attention immediately. The National Resident Matching Program reports that letters of recommendation are consistently ranked among the top factors program directors consider when selecting applicants to interview.

The body of the letter should provide detailed clinical evidence to support the recommendation. Rather than simply listing qualities like "hardworking" or "intelligent," the writer should describe specific clinical encounters, patient presentations, or team situations where the student demonstrated those qualities. For example, instead of saying the student has good clinical judgment, describe a specific case where the student’s assessment and management plan were accurate and appropriate, especially if it was a complex or challenging case.

Residency program directors look for evidence of the six ACGME core competencies: patient care, medical knowledge, practice-based learning and improvement, interpersonal and communication skills, professionalism, and systems-based practice. A strong letter addresses several of these competencies with concrete examples. However, the letter does not need to address all six comprehensively. It is better to give a few detailed, specific examples that genuinely reflect the student’s strengths than to superficially cover every competency.

A comparative statement is one of the most important elements of a strong residency recommendation letter. Program directors want to know how this student compares to others the writer has supervised. A statement like "in the top 10 percent of students I have worked with over the past decade" is far more informative than "a very good medical student." The more specific the comparison, the better. If the writer has supervised many students over many years, their comparative assessment carries more weight.

Medical students can help their letter writers produce stronger letters by providing them with helpful materials. This typically includes an updated CV, a draft of their personal statement, a summary of their clinical experience with that particular attending, and any specific programs or specialties they are targeting. Some students also provide a short list of accomplishments or memorable moments from the rotation that they hope the writer will mention. However, students should never write the letter themselves or put words in the writer’s mouth.

The closing paragraph should summarize the writer’s overall assessment and give a clear, strong recommendation. It can also mention the writer’s confidence that the student will succeed in residency and beyond. Offer to provide additional information if needed. The letter should be on official institutional letterhead, signed by the writer, and submitted through ERAS or the program’s preferred application system. Aim for a length of one to two pages.

When using a letter of recommendation residency template, it is important to remember that the letter represents you as a medical professional. Make sure the letter is professional, well-written, and free of errors. Ask a trusted mentor or colleague to review it before you submit it, if possible. The quality of your recommendation letters reflects on you as an applicant and on the physicians who wrote them, so taking the time to get them right is worth the effort.

Medical students should also think strategically about who they ask for recommendation letters. It is better to have a slightly less famous attending who knows you well and can write a detailed, enthusiastic letter, than a big name who can only write a generic, lukewarm one. Program directors can tell the difference, and a detailed letter from someone who worked closely with you will always be more impressive than a short form letter from a department chair you barely know.

Final review checklist

  • Opening establishes credentials, relationship, and strong endorsement.
  • Specific clinical examples demonstrate key competencies.
  • A comparative ranking quantifies the student’s performance.
  • The letter addresses competencies relevant to the target specialty.
  • Letter is on letterhead, signed, and submitted through the proper system.

Frequently asked questions

What should a residency letter of recommendation include?

A residency letter of recommendation should include the writer’s relationship to the applicant, the duration and clinical setting of their work together, specific examples of the student’s clinical skills and personal qualities, a comparative ranking, and a clear statement of recommendation.

How many letters do I need for residency?

Most residency programs require three letters of recommendation, though some require four. Choose clinical attendings who know you well from rotations in your target specialty or core clerkships where you performed well.

Do residency programs actually read all the letters?

Yes, program directors and selection committees read recommendation letters carefully. Studies by the NRMP consistently rank letters of recommendation among the most important factors in selecting applicants for interviews, especially for competitive specialties.
