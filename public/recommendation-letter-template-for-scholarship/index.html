Writing guide

How to use a recommendation letter template for scholarship

A recommendation letter template for scholarship applications helps teachers, professors, counselors, and mentors write persuasive, well-structured reference letters for students applying for scholarships, fellowships, and financial aid. Scholarship committees review hundreds or even thousands of applications, and a strong recommendation letter can make the difference between an application that gets noticed and one that blends in with the crowd. A good template ensures that the letter includes all the information scholarship committees look for.

Scholarship recommendation letters serve a unique purpose. Unlike college admissions letters, which focus on overall academic potential and personal character, scholarship letters often need to align with the specific mission and values of the scholarship organization. Some scholarships reward academic excellence, others focus on community service or leadership, and still others support students from specific backgrounds or fields of study. The best recommendation letters are tailored to the particular scholarship and highlight the qualities the scholarship values most.

The strongest scholarship recommendation letters provide specific evidence rather than generic praise. For example, instead of saying the student is "a natural leader," describe a specific project or initiative the student led, what challenges they faced, how they motivated others, and what the outcome was. Numbers and specific details make the letter more credible and memorable. According to the Federal Student Aid office, scholarships are a critical source of funding for millions of college students each year.

When writing a scholarship recommendation letter, start by understanding what the scholarship is looking for. Review the scholarship’s website, application materials, and selection criteria. If the student provides you with a description of the scholarship, read it carefully. Then structure your letter to highlight the student’s strengths in the areas the scholarship emphasizes. If the scholarship is about academic excellence, focus on the student’s intellectual curiosity and academic achievements. If it is about community service, focus on their volunteer work and commitment to others.

The structure of a scholarship recommendation letter typically follows a clear pattern. The opening paragraph introduces you, your relationship to the student, and how long you have known them, and gives an overall assessment of the student’s suitability for the scholarship. The body paragraphs provide specific examples that demonstrate the student’s strengths. The closing paragraph summarizes your recommendation and offers to provide more information if needed. Aim for a length of about one page, or 400 to 600 words.

Students can help their recommenders write stronger scholarship letters by providing them with useful information. Give your recommender a copy of the scholarship description and application details. Provide your resume or a list of your activities, achievements, and goals. Remind them of specific experiences or projects you worked on together that might be good examples to include. If there are particular qualities or achievements you would like them to emphasize, mention them politely. But remember, the letter should be the recommender’s own honest opinion.

Before submitting a scholarship recommendation letter, proofread it carefully. Check for spelling and grammar errors, and make sure all names, dates, and details are correct. The letter should be on official letterhead if possible, especially if it is from a school or organization. It should be signed and dated, and it should include your full name, title, and contact information. Make sure you submit the letter by the deadline, using the submission method specified by the scholarship organization.

When using a recommendation letter template for scholarship, it is important to tailor each letter to the specific scholarship you are applying for. Different scholarships emphasize different qualities, and the recommendation letter should highlight what that particular scholarship values most. A scholarship for academic excellence should focus on grades and intellectual curiosity. A community service scholarship should focus on volunteer work and leadership. Sending the same generic letter to every scholarship is unlikely to be as effective as taking the time to customize each one.

Students should also keep track of all the scholarships they apply for and which recommenders they have asked for each one. Create a spreadsheet with scholarship names, deadlines, requirements, and the status of each recommendation letter. This helps avoid missed deadlines, prevents asking the same person for too many letters at once, and makes it easy to send thank-you notes after all the letters are submitted. Organization is key when applying for multiple scholarships.

Final review checklist

  • The letter is tailored to the specific scholarship’s criteria.
  • Specific examples demonstrate the student’s relevant qualities.
  • The writer’s relationship and credentials are clearly stated.
  • The letter addresses the scholarship’s mission and values.
  • The letter is on letterhead, signed, and submitted on time.

Frequently asked questions

What should a scholarship recommendation letter include?

A scholarship recommendation letter should include the writer’s relationship to the student, specific examples of the student’s achievements and qualities relevant to the scholarship, a clear statement of support, and the writer’s contact information.

Who should write a scholarship recommendation letter?

Scholarship recommendation letters are typically written by teachers, professors, school counselors, mentors, coaches, employers, or community leaders who know the student well and can speak to their achievements, character, and potential.

How long should a scholarship recommendation letter be?

A scholarship recommendation letter is typically about one page or 400 to 600 words long. It should be long enough to provide specific examples and a strong endorsement, but concise enough to hold the reader’s attention.
