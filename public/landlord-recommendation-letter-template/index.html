Writing guide

How to use a landlord recommendation letter template

A landlord recommendation letter template provides property owners and managers with a ready-to-use format for verifying a tenant’s rental history. When prospective tenants apply for new housing, they typically need to provide previous landlord contact information so the new landlord can verify their rental track record. A well-written recommendation from a prior landlord can significantly strengthen a rental application and help tenants qualify for properties they want.

The core of any landlord recommendation letter is verifiable rental information. This includes the exact address of the rental property, the move-in and move-out dates, the monthly rent amount, and the security deposit amount. Most importantly, it should state whether the tenant paid rent consistently and on time. According to the National Apartment Association, payment history is the number one factor landlords evaluate when screening new tenants.

A comprehensive landlord recommendation goes beyond just financial information. It can also cover whether the tenant gave proper written notice before vacating, whether they caused any damage beyond normal wear and tear, whether they were respectful of neighbors and property rules, and how they communicated with management. These behavioral details give the next landlord confidence about what kind of tenant the applicant will be.

When using a landlord recommendation letter template, always make sure the information is accurate and up to date. Double-check dates, rent amounts, and names. If you cannot remember specific details, look them up in your records rather than guessing. Inaccurate information can harm both the tenant and your credibility as a reference. If the tenant had issues during their tenancy, be honest but fair, focusing on facts rather than opinions.

Landlords should also be aware of fair housing laws when writing recommendation letters. The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. Your recommendation letter should focus only on tenancy-related facts and should not mention any protected characteristics, even if you think they are positive. If you are unsure whether something is appropriate to include, stick to verifiable facts about rent payment, lease compliance, and property care.

For tenants requesting a recommendation from their landlord, timing is important. Ask for the letter at least one to two weeks before you need it. Provide your landlord with any specific forms or formats the new property requires. If you had a good tenancy, briefly mention highlights to jog their memory, such as consistently paying rent early or being a considerate neighbor. Always thank your landlord for taking the time to write the letter.

Whether you are a landlord providing a reference or a tenant requesting one, having a high-quality landlord recommendation letter template ensures consistency and professionalism. The template should include all standard information while leaving room for customization based on each tenant’s unique situation. A well-crafted rental reference can make a real difference in helping good tenants secure the housing they want.

For property managers who handle multiple rental references, having a standardized landlord recommendation letter template process can save significant time. Consider creating both a short version for basic verification and a longer version for detailed recommendations, so you can quickly provide the right level of detail for each request. Keep records of all reference letters you provide, including the date, the tenant’s name, and what information was shared, in case questions arise later.

Tenants should also understand that not all landlord references will be glowing. If you had some late payments or other issues during your tenancy, do not panic. Be upfront with potential new landlords about what happened and what you have learned from the experience. A honest explanation paired with evidence of improved behavior can often be more persuasive than a perfect rental history from someone who has never faced challenges.

Final review checklist

  • Full property address and exact tenancy dates are included.
  • Monthly rent amount and payment history are clearly stated.
  • Lease compliance and property condition details are factual.
  • Fair Housing guidelines are followed (no protected characteristics).
  • Landlord name, title, contact info, and signature are present.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a landlord recommendation letter?

Start with the tenant’s name, property address, and tenancy dates. State the monthly rent and whether rent was paid on time. Mention lease compliance and property condition. Close with a clear recommendation and your contact information.

What makes a good landlord reference letter?

A good landlord reference letter is factual, specific, and professional. It includes verifiable details about rent payment history, tenancy dates, and the tenant’s overall responsibility, and it is written on official letterhead when possible.

How long does a landlord reference take?

Most landlords can provide a basic reference within a few business days. For a more detailed recommendation letter, give your landlord at least one to two weeks’ notice to ensure they have time to write it properly.
