Common recommendation letter questions

Find direct answers about who should write a recommendation, ideal length, ethical self-drafting, strong evidence, and the difference between references and recommendations.

Who Should Write a Recommendation Letter

The practical answer to "who should write a recommendation letter" depends on the application rules, the relationship, and the evidence available. In most situations, the best approach is to choose credibility and useful evidence over title alone. Focus on relationship quality, relevance, recency, examples, enthusiasm, and eligibility rules. A decision should be based on what the recipient requests and what the recommender can honestly verify.

A useful example is work or study the potential recommender personally observed. Avoid choosing a prestigious person who barely knows the applicant. Applicants can provide deadlines, instructions, resumes, and reminders of shared work, but the final letter should remain accurate and should be reviewed by the person with the most relevant, direct, and recent knowledge. When a school, employer, court, government agency, or application platform gives specific instructions, those requirements take priority over general guidance.
